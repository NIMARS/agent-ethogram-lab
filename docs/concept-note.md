# Public Concept Note

Status: conceptual draft; release review pending

Updated: 2026-10-29

## Concept

LLM-based agents can produce different observable behavior as an interaction
develops and as their surrounding configuration changes. Evaluating only one
isolated answer may therefore miss behavior that appears through accumulated
context, repeated choices, corrections, or changes in available information.

Agent Ethogram Lab explores an ethogram-inspired way to document such behavior.
In this public concept, an **agent ethogram** is simply a structured account of
what an agent did, the conditions under which it was observed, and the evidence
available to the evaluator.

This is a working research lens, not a claim that software is an animal, a
person, or a conscious subject.

## Why explore it

A structured record may help researchers ask more precise questions about:

- whether an observation repeats under comparable conditions;
- whether a change comes from the agent, its context, or the measurement
  process;
- how much evidence is needed before describing a behavioral pattern;
- how uncertainty and limitations should accompany a comparison.

Whether this framing is useful, sufficiently distinct from existing evaluation
approaches, or scientifically valid remains an open question for literature
review and empirical study.

## Public principles

- Describe observable outputs and actions, not presumed inner states.
- Separate proposals, observations, evaluations, and interpretations.
- State the conditions and limits attached to a finding.
- Prefer reproducible evidence over memorable examples.
- Report uncertainty, disagreement, negative results, and known confounds.
- Do not turn a research measurement into a claim of general safety or
  certification.

## Current stage

The project now has a local research prototype and a limited exploratory pilot
with maintainer review. This development progress does not establish the
scientific validity or general usefulness of the proposed framing.

This candidate contains no software implementation, benchmark, dataset, study
records, or results. The present note describes the research direction only;
its exact text remains subject to release review.
