# Candidate Public Research Questions

Status: provisional  
Updated: 2026-07-28

These are high-level candidate questions for public discussion. They are not
claims of novelty, preregistered hypotheses, or evidence that a particular
method works. The set may change after literature review and feasibility
assessment.

1. How does observable agent behavior change across an extended interaction
   compared with isolated responses?
2. Which contextual and configuration details are necessary to interpret and
   reproduce a behavioral observation?
3. How can repeated observations distinguish a persistent pattern from
   stochastic variation or a measurement artifact?
4. How should human judgment and automated evaluation be compared, calibrated,
   and reported when they disagree?
5. How can researchers distinguish a change in the evaluated system from a
   change in prompts, tools, available information, or evaluation conditions?
6. What forms of evidence make a behavioral comparison useful for diagnosis
   without overstating generality?
7. How should findings be communicated without attributing consciousness,
   feelings, or intentions to an LLM?

## Selection gate

Before empirical work is described as a study, a smaller question set should be
selected and connected to:

- a clearly defined unit of observation;
- an appropriate comparison or baseline;
- measurable outcomes and uncertainty;
- available, ethically usable data;
- conditions that could contradict the expectation;
- a publication and reproducibility plan.

Detailed instruments and operational choices are outside the present public
baseline.
